Why the timing works

The moment

They need loads this week

New carriers' first problem is not paperwork — it is finding freight. A dispatch offer lands differently in week one than it does in month six, when habits and relationships are set.

Equipment fit

Call only the trucks you can load

The cargo field (general freight, autos, produce, household goods and more) plus power units lets you target exactly the equipment you dispatch — one-truck dry vans, reefer teams, flatbed fleets.

Owner-direct

The phone reaches the decision-maker

These are one-to-five-truck businesses. The filed phone is the owner's — frequently the cell, flagged as such in the file — not a call center or a gatekeeper.

How dispatch services work the file

Daily recruiting list

Each morning's grants are your call list: new trucks in your lanes, sorted by equipment and fleet size, hours before mid-morning digest services deliver.

Win the first month

Offer the first-week load plan while the carrier is still setting up. A truck you load in week one tends to stay your truck.

Re-engage at renewal

The renewals file flags carriers approaching a policy anniversary — a natural "review your costs" moment when a dispatch pitch about net revenue lands well.

Per-lead math

All States is $49/mo for all 5,199 of the last 30 days' grants — about 0.9¢ per new carrier. One retained truck covers the year.

What's in every row

Sixteen fields, insurer and BIPD effective date first: insurer name, policy effective date, legal name and DBA, USDOT and MC numbers, street/city/state/ZIP, phone (cell-flagged), email, power units, cargo/equipment, and the authority grant date.
