Why new authorities are the best-timed leads in trucking insurance

An operating-authority grant is not a "maybe someday" prospect. It is a business that just cleared a federal checklist whose last step is buying the exact policy you sell — and a business whose renewal date is now on a clock you can read.

The grant

They must buy before they can roll

No active BIPD liability filing, no legal operation. Every carrier in the daily file has either just bound coverage — and can still be quoted against it — or is finalizing it this week. You are calling during the decision, not after it.

The renewal

You know their X-date

Every plan includes the renewal file: carriers whose liability policy anniversary falls within the next 60 days, with their current insurer named. That is a 60-day head start on every requote in your state.

The lapse

A lapsed filing is an emergency

When a liability filing is cancelled and not replaced, the carrier's authority is headed for revocation. The lapse file lists them while fixing it is their most urgent problem — and a new policy is the fix.

What's in every row

One row per carrier, sixteen fields, ordered the way agents actually work a list: current insurer and BIPD effective date first — who has the policy now, and when it renews — then the carrier and its contacts. Compiled from FMCSA's public Licensing & Insurance and company census records. No padding, no recycled lists — the file is rebuilt from the federal source on every run.

Questions, answered straight

Where does the data come from?

FMCSA's own public records — the Licensing & Insurance files and the motor carrier census, published by the U.S. Department of Transportation. FreshAuthority compiles, cleans, deduplicates, and delivers them in a workable format. The underlying records are public; you're paying for the daily compilation, the trigger files, and the delivery, not for the raw data.

How current is it?

The grants file is rebuilt from the latest FMCSA release every delivery morning (Monday–Saturday) and delivered by 6:00 AM Eastern — up to three hours before services that deliver around 8:00 AM Central. A grant that posts to FMCSA's system is in your file the next delivery cycle — typically within one business day of the grant itself.

How is the renewal date calculated? FMCSA doesn't publish expirations.

Correct — and any seller who claims otherwise is guessing quietly. We use the industry-standard method: a policy's renewal falls on the anniversary of its liability filing's effective date. The renewals file lists carriers whose anniversary falls within the next 60 days, filtered to policies at least 300 days old. It's a proxy — an accurate, openly-described one.

What counts as a "lapse"?

A carrier whose liability (BIPD) filing shows a cancellation effective within the last 30 days and who has no active replacement filing in the current insurance file. Those carriers face authority revocation if they don't re-file — which is why the list converts.

Some listed contacts belong to a filing service, not the carrier. What about that?

Straight answer: it happens, on every list built from FMCSA filings. The contact on a row is whoever the carrier put on its federal filings, and some carriers file through a compliance or insurance-filing service — so occasionally the phone or email belongs to that service rather than to the carrier itself. We're adding a contact_type flag to the file that marks contacts appearing across many unrelated carriers as likely filing-agent contacts; that flag is in development and is not in the file yet, and we'd rather say so plainly than pretend the problem doesn't exist. Every other field on the row — carrier, address, insurer, dates — is still the carrier's own.

Are the phone numbers cell phones?

Every row carries the phone number the carrier filed. The phone_type field flags it as a cell when the number matches the carrier's census cell-phone field. Numbers that match only the business phone field are marked business/unknown — we flag what we can verify and label the rest honestly.
